# Color Palette

The dzup-ui palette is authored in **OKLCH** for perceptually-uniform lightness:
a `-500` step reads as the same visual weight across every hue, and dark-mode
inversions stay legible. Every swatch below reads **live** from a `var(--dz-*)`
custom property, so this page can never drift from the shipped tokens — flip the
**Theme** toolbar to watch the semantic blocks remap.

Two layers make up the system:

1. **Primitive hues** — raw `50 → 950` ramps. Split into _intent_ palettes
   (wired to semantic roles) and a _decorative_ spectrum (for charts, tags,
   illustrations, and richer block/template design).
2. **Semantic roles** — intent-based aliases (`--dz-primary`, `--dz-danger`, …)
   that remap per theme. **These are what you use in app and component code.**

> Rule of thumb: reach for a **semantic** token first. Drop to a **primitive**
> step only when no semantic role expresses your intent (e.g. a categorical
> chart series or a decorative gradient). Never hardcode hex / Tailwind colors
> (ADR-04).

## Intent palettes

These seven ramps back the semantic roles. `primary`/`secondary` carry the brand;
`success`/`warning`/`danger`/`info` are the status family; `neutral` is the
canonical gray used for surfaces, borders and text.

<PaletteGrid names={['primary', 'secondary', 'neutral', 'success', 'warning', 'danger', 'info']} />

## Decorative spectrum

A Tailwind-aligned spectrum for data visualization, tags, avatars, illustrations
and marketing blocks. Hues are spaced ~15–20° apart around the OKLCH wheel so
adjacent categorical series stay visually distinct. They carry **no** semantic
meaning — `--dz-colors-rose-500` is just a color, not "danger".

<PaletteGrid names={['rose', 'red', 'orange', 'amber', 'yellow', 'lime', 'green', 'emerald', 'teal', 'cyan', 'sky', 'blue', 'indigo', 'violet', 'purple', 'fuchsia', 'pink']} />

### Neutral undertones

`neutral` is the default gray. These add a **cool** (`slate`), a **near-pure**
(`zinc`) and a **warm** (`stone`) ramp so a brand can give its surfaces a subtle
temperature without leaving the token system.

<PaletteGrid names={['neutral', 'slate', 'zinc', 'stone']} />

## Per-intent token reference

Every intent below exposes this consistent set of roles.

<DocTable
  head={['Token', 'Role', 'Light', 'Dark']}
  align={['left', 'left', 'left', 'left']}
  rows={[
    ['`--dz-{intent}`', 'Solid fill', 'shade 500', 'shade 400'],
    ['`--dz-{intent}-foreground`', 'Text on the solid fill', 'white', 'shade 950'],
    ['`--dz-{intent}-hover`', 'Solid fill, hovered', 'shade 600', 'shade 300'],
    ['`--dz-{intent}-active`', 'Solid fill, pressed', 'shade 700', 'shade 200'],
    ['`--dz-{intent}-muted`', 'Subtle tinted bg (badges, alerts)', 'shade 100', 'shade 900'],
    ['`--dz-{intent}-muted-foreground`', 'Emphasis text on the subtle bg', 'shade 700', 'shade 300'],
    ['`--dz-{intent}-border`', 'Intent-tinted border', 'shade 200', 'shade 800'],
  ]}
/>

> `warning` is the exception: solid fills use the dedicated `--dz-warning-solid`
> pair (a bright yellow with dark text) instead of a dark fill, in both themes.
